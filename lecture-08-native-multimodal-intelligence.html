Chapter 8 · Victoria Lin · May 21, 2026

From Language Models to Native Multimodal Intelligence

A common transformer can connect words, pictures, sound, and actions without giving them identical representations or training objectives. The central design problem is to preserve the information each task needs while letting the modalities condition one another.

This chapter follows Victoria Lin’s CS25 V6 lecture and its question period. Lin identifies the talk as public material and personal views. The original models cited below establish particular architectures and experiments; the maintenance case, proofs, cost calculations, program, and exercises are added teaching constructions. Approximate timestamps follow a local machine transcript, not publisher captions. The interaction-model discussion is a separately marked extension from the official deck.

1. Language is a powerful interface with a limited view 0:04

A language model can manipulate a description of the world while missing what the description leaves out. A manual may say that a connector has a retaining clip; a photograph shows whether the clip is bent. A transcript records words but may discard hesitation, emphasis, or a warning sound. The motivation for multimodal modeling is to use these observations directly, rather than require a human to convert all of them into prose before the model can reason about them.

Carry a maintenance assistant through the chapter. An operator supplies a manual revision, a photo of a component, and a spoken question. The assistant must identify the relevant part, answer using the correct revision, and optionally draw an annotated diagram. These are three distinct outputs: a claim about the observed component, a grounded textual answer, and a generated visual explanation. An attractive diagram can be wrong about the part; a correct caption does not establish that its arrows are correctly placed.

Call a model multimodal when it processes more than one kind of signal. Call a generative model native multimodal here when the modalities participate in the model’s joint sequence processing and generation design, rather than being treated only as a separate service reached through a textual command. This is a design description, not a single standardized certification. An image encoder attached to a language model can be useful without generating images; an early-fusion model can still contain modality-specific encoders, embeddings, and output heads.

Lin distinguishes digital-world applications from perception and control in the physical world. The maintenance case straddles that boundary: documents and diagrams are digital, but the component’s condition is physical. The observation must retain provenance, time, and uncertainty. Seeing a part through a camera is evidence about the scene, not authorization to operate machinery. Adding an action output changes the application contract as well as the tensor format.

2. A token is a sequence position, not necessarily a word ID 4:19

For text, a tokenizer often maps a string to discrete vocabulary indices. Looking up each index yields an embedding in $\mathbb R^d$, where $d$ is the transformer’s hidden width. An image can instead be split into patches and each patch mapped to a dense vector. A continuous patch vector is a token in the sequence-processing sense even though it has no unique vocabulary index. The transformer consumes a matrix $X\in\mathbb R^{n\times d}$ of $n$ vectors; the route by which each row was produced remains modality-specific.

For a deliberately simple spatial tokenizer, pad an image of height $H$ and width $W$ to multiples of patch side $p$. Its count is $n_I=\lceil H/p\rceil\lceil W/p\rceil$. Treating $F$ video frames independently gives $Fn_I$ positions. This is an accounting model with no temporal compression, crops, thumbnails, boundary tokens, or pooling. At $512\times512$ and $p=16$, one image gives $1024$ positions. Twelve frames give $12{,}288$. Changing the tokenizer changes these numbers; “a video token” does not identify a universal duration or pixel area.

Suppose the manual and question use $512$ text positions. With one image, $n=1536$; with twelve frames, $n=12{,}800$. Explicitly storing one attention score matrix in two-byte elements costs $2n^2$ bytes: $4.5$ MiB and $312.5$ MiB respectively, where $1$ MiB is $2^{20}$ bytes. These are per-head, per-example matrix sizes, not total activation memory or a statement that an efficient attention kernel materializes the matrix. Streaming attention can avoid that storage while still doing work on the permitted query–key pairs.

Audio representations may preserve waveform details, a time-frequency transform, semantic content, speaker identity, or combinations of them. A sequence of phonetic or semantic codes is not equivalent to a sequence that retains prosody and environmental sounds. The engineering question is what information survives the front end. If the maintenance assistant needs to distinguish a rattling bearing from normal operation, transcribing the operator’s words alone may discard the signal that matters.

3. Separate multimodal inputs from multimodal outputs 7:21

A model that receives a photograph and emits a caption has multimodal inputs and text outputs. It can train with a text cross-entropy objective even though the conditioning context contains image features. Writing $y_t$ for the discrete target word and $c$ for the observed image and previous text, the negative log likelihood is $-\log p_\theta(y_t\mid c,y_{<t})$. There is no requirement to reconstruct the image merely because it occurs in the context.

That does not imply that the image encoder receives no training signal. Consider an original scalar illustration: an image encoder produces $u$, the text head’s binary logit is $z=au$, and the target is $y\in\{0,1\}$. With $\sigma(z)=1/(1+e^{-z})$, the text loss is $\ell=-y\log\sigma(z)-(1-y)\log(1-\sigma(z))$. Differentiation gives $\partial\ell/\partial u=a(\sigma(au)-y)$. At $a=u=y=1$, the loss is $0.313262$ nats and this derivative is $-0.268941$. A loss on a text target can therefore teach an image feature through the conditioning path. Freezing that encoder or explicitly stopping its gradient would change the conclusion.

To generate an image, the system also needs a distribution over an image representation and a mechanism to decode it. It might generate discrete image codes autoregressively, predict noise in a continuous latent image, or use another continuous generative objective. Speech output similarly needs an acoustic representation and a decoder, not just a textual transcript of the intended utterance. “Text-only loss” and “text-only input” must stay separate in an architecture description.

For the assistant, a captioning test evaluates only part of the desired behavior. A correct label for the component does not establish that the model can draw its retaining clip, preserve a warning label, or render the manual’s exact revision identifier. Conversely, image-generation quality does not establish factual correctness. Lin’s contemporary product examples illustrate the expanding output space; the chapter’s technical claims concern the checked architectures, without treating a product name as evidence of all capabilities.

4. Reuse language-model tools with new measurement contracts 9:06

Prompting, in-context examples, intermediate reasoning, data scaling, and sparse experts extend naturally to multimodal systems. Their meanings nevertheless change with the output. An image can show an exemplar arrangement, not just describe it. A prompt can contain a photo that fixes the scene and words that specify the edit. Intermediate text can resolve an ambiguous request before generation, but it can also introduce a mistaken interpretation that the image renderer faithfully follows.

In the maintenance case, a textual plan might state “label the left connector and preserve the serial number.” That plan is useful only if “left” is anchored to the displayed orientation and the model locates the correct connector. Evaluate the interpretation and the generated diagram separately. A fluent plan is observable output; it is not a privileged account of the model’s internal mechanism. If the plan gets a part identity wrong, longer reasoning alone may amplify the wrong condition.

Likewise, a scaling comparison must specify what grows. More text tokens, more images, larger image resolution, more frames, and more total transformer parameters are different interventions. Equal token counts can contain very different numbers of examples. Sampling at a fixed image-to-text position ratio while reducing positions per image lets a model see more images; this changes the information exposure even when nominal token budgets match.

Lin treats multimodal scaling and cross-modal transfer as active research questions. It would be too strong to say that no scaling studies exist: Transfusion and MoT contain explicit controlled comparisons. It would also be too strong to infer a universal law from those studies. Record the representation, objective, active and stored parameters, data mixture, resolution, sequence length, and evaluation direction. The next sections use this discipline to distinguish three architectural choices.

5. Chameleon makes pictures part of a discrete sequence 11:41

Chameleon trains a mixed-modal autoregressive transformer from scratch. Images are encoded into discrete codes and interleaved with text. A shared output vocabulary allows the model to predict the next text or image code, so captions, images, and mixed documents use the same sequence factorization. Chameleon v1, PDF pp. 4–8, records the original tokenizer, training mixture, and stability modifications. Its $512\times512$ images become $1024$ discrete codes from an $8192$-entry image codebook; those codes are part of a larger vocabulary.

Vector quantization makes this conversion precise. An encoder produces a latent vector $z\in\mathbb R^r$, and a codebook contains prototypes $e_1,\ldots,e_K\in\mathbb R^r$. Choose $k(z)=\arg\min_j\|z-e_j\|_2$ with a specified tie rule, transmit index $k$, and let the decoder use $e_k$. A grid of such indices becomes an ordered sequence. The original VQ-VAE-2 account explicitly treats this as potentially lossy communication. Its v1 PDF p. 2 establishes the nearest-prototype operation; the finite examples here do not reproduce its trained encoder or decoder.

The autoregressive probability factorization is $p_\theta(s_{1:n})=\prod_{i=1}^n p_\theta(s_i\mid s_{<i})$ for discrete sequence $s$. The representation gives a vocabulary-based likelihood and ordinary next-token decoding, but it also imposes an ordering over image codes. That ordering is a modeling choice, not a statement that the physical image is naturally read as a sentence. At generation time, the code sequence must be decoded into pixels.

In the assistant, interleaving would allow “here is the relevant clip” followed by an image and an explanation. Training examples with both orders matter: text before image teaches image generation conditioned on text; image before text teaches description conditioned on an image. A single mathematical factorization supports both, but neither direction appears automatically without data and optimization. Chameleon’s training also required attention/output stability interventions. Sharing the sequence interface is not the same as making optimization trivial.

6. Reconstruction quality and task sufficiency are different 15:31

Quantization can erase distinctions. Let a scalar latent take values $0.1$ and $0.2$, each with probability $1/2$, and let the codebook be $\{0,1\}$. Both values map to code $0$. Assign task label $Y=0$ to the first and $Y=1$ to the second. Any deterministic classifier that sees only that code returns the same answer for both, so its error is at least $1/2$. Randomization cannot improve the expected error: conditioned on the shared code the labels remain equally likely. An encoder that preserves the original scalar permits a perfect threshold classifier.

This is an information-loss counterexample, not a general ranking of continuous and discrete models. A learned discrete representation can preserve the distinctions a task needs; a continuous low-dimensional representation can also discard them. The test is whether the retained representation is sufficient for the target. Formally, a representation $Z$ loses no target-relevant information relative to observation $X$ when $p(Y\mid X)=p(Y\mid Z)$ on the evaluated distribution. This does not demand pixel-perfect reconstruction.

A maintenance photograph can lose a tiny digit while remaining visually convincing. In the opposite direction, a semantic encoder can preserve the part identity while discarding the exact surface texture needed for realistic reconstruction. These are useful tradeoffs for different objectives. Chameleon’s paper explicitly records weaknesses in reconstructing text-heavy images; that is a measured limitation of its tokenizer, not proof that every discrete tokenizer fails OCR.

Lin connects discretization to generation efficiency and visual understanding. Preserve that motivation without declaring that quantization alone causes every performance gap. Decoder quality, latent resolution, stability changes, training data, and optimization all vary in real systems. A controlled comparison can reduce confounding; a toy collision proves only the narrower proposition that a noninjective map can destroy a particular label distinction.

7. Transfusion combines discrete text with continuous image generation 16:50

Transfusion uses a common transformer with cross-entropy for discrete text and a diffusion objective for continuous image latents. A pretrained variational autoencoder maps pixels to a compact latent tensor; patch projections map that tensor into transformer vectors. Calling the latents continuous means that the model avoids a codebook quantization step. It does not mean that the VAE preserves every pixel detail or that compression is lossless.

Use $\tau$ for diffusion time, reserving sequence position $i$ for token order. A standard forward noise construction in Transfusion v1, PDF pp. 4–6, is $x_\tau=\sqrt{\bar\alpha_\tau}x_0+\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_\tau}\epsilon$, with $\epsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,I)$ and $0<\bar\alpha_\tau<1$. Here $x_0\in\mathbb R^r$ is the clean latent image and $\bar\alpha_\tau$ is a scalar noise-schedule coefficient. A model predicts the added noise from the corrupted image, diffusion time, and preceding multimodal context $c$.

The denoising loss has the form $L_I=\mathbb E\|\epsilon-\epsilon_\theta(x_\tau,\tau,c)\|_2^2$. Text positions use $L_T=-\mathbb E\log p_\theta(y_i\mid c,y_{<i})$. Combining them as $L=L_T+\lambda L_I$ introduces a balancing coefficient $\lambda$, not a unit-free guarantee of balanced learning. Per-token, per-image, and per-latent-coordinate reductions affect the relative gradient scale. The paper computes text loss per token and diffusion loss per image; its selected coefficient belongs to that implementation.

Generation switches modes. After a beginning-of-image marker, the decoder inserts a noisy latent image and repeatedly predicts denoising updates. It replaces the image’s current noisy state rather than appending every diffusion step as a new visible image. After the image is complete, an end marker returns the sequence to text generation. The assistant can thus generate an image between two passages of prose without treating every latent coordinate as a vocabulary class. The reverse-process sampler, noise schedule, and image decoder are essential pieces; the finite program below implements none of them.

Denoising predicts a conditional mean, not a unique hidden truth

For any square-integrable random noise vector $E$ and observed conditioning variable $U$, let $m(U)=\mathbb E[E\mid U]$. Expand the squared error for a square-integrable deterministic predictor $f(U)$:

$$\mathbb E\|E-f(U)\|^2=\mathbb E\|E-m(U)\|^2+\mathbb E\|m(U)-f(U)\|^2.$$

The cross term vanishes because $\mathbb E[E-m(U)\mid U]=0$. Therefore $m$ minimizes mean squared error, with irreducible residual uncertainty unless the observation determines the noise. This original derivation explains the objective; it does not show that a finite neural network learns the optimum or that taking one conditional mean generates the desired image distribution.

For an analytic scalar boundary check, take independent $x_0,\epsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,1)$ and $x_\tau=\sqrt\alpha x_0+\sqrt{1-\alpha}\epsilon$. Joint Gaussian conditioning gives $\mathbb E[\epsilon\mid x_\tau]=\sqrt{1-\alpha}\,x_\tau$ and conditional variance $\alpha$. At $\alpha=1/4$ and observed $x_\tau=2$, the optimal noise prediction is $\sqrt3\approx1.732051$, and residual MSE is $1/4$. The noisy observation still admits multiple clean states. The diagram generator’s uncertainty cannot be eliminated merely by changing a loss label from cross-entropy to MSE.

Permit image-local bidirectionality without seeing future context

Images benefit from attention across patches of the same image. The Transfusion mask allows an image patch to attend to all patches in its own image, while maintaining causal order between that image and the surrounding text or other images. Let $b(i)$ be an image-block identifier when position $i$ is an image patch and be undefined for text. Permit key $j$ for query $i$ when $j\le i$ or when both positions belong to the same image block. The blocks must each be contiguous in this simplified representation. Beginning/end markers need their own implementation policy and are omitted from the finite fixture.

For positions $[T,I_0,I_0,T,I_1,I_1,T]$, query position $1$ can read patch $2$ in the same image even though $2>1$. It cannot read the later text at $3$ or later image $I_1$. The text at $3$ can read the first image but not the second. This is global causal block order with local bidirectionality; it is not unrestricted attention over all image patches in the document.

The same property must survive multiple layers. No position in an earlier text/image block is allowed to obtain information from a later block, including through an intermediate position. Since every allowed cross-block edge points to the current or an earlier block, composing layers cannot create a path from a later block into an earlier one. A mistaken mask that gives all image patches bidirectional access breaks this argument. It could make a training caption exploit an image that the model has not generated yet.

For the assistant, a prior reference image can condition a generated diagram. The generated diagram cannot secretly read the answer text that follows it. The finite program changes later-block values and verifies that the earlier prefix is unchanged. It also verifies a positive case: same-image patches can communicate. Testing only that a forbidden entry is zero would miss an implementation that accidentally blocks all useful attention.

8. Mixture-of-Transformers separates weights while sharing attention 20:57

Mixture-of-Transformers, or MoT, selects modality-specific transformer parameters deterministically. For row vector $x_i\in\mathbb R^{1\times d}$ and assigned branch $m_i$, define $q_i=x_iW_Q^{m_i}$, $k_i=x_iW_K^{m_i}$, and $v_i=x_iW_V^{m_i}$. With $W_Q^m,W_K^m\in\mathbb R^{d\times d_k}$ and $W_V^m\in\mathbb R^{d\times d_v}$, restoring the original position order yields $Q,K\in\mathbb R^{n\times d_k}$ and $V\in\mathbb R^{n\times d_v}$.

$$A_{ij}=\frac{\exp(q_i k_j^{\mathsf T}/\sqrt{d_k})}{\sum_{u\in J_i}\exp(q_i k_u^{\mathsf T}/\sqrt{d_k})},\quad j\in J_i;\qquad o_i=\Big(\sum_{j\in J_i} A_{ij}v_j\Big)W_O^{m_i},$$

Here $J_i$ is the permitted-key set, $A_{ij}=0$ outside it, and $W_O^m\in\mathbb R^{d_v\times d}$. The normalization covers all permitted keys across modalities. The subsequent feed-forward network and normalization parameters are also branch-specific. MoT v2, PDF pp. 6–9, establishes this sequence: group positions, apply branch-specific projections, restore order, perform global attention, and apply branch-specific output/FFN processing.

The important communication happens in the attention sum. A text query can assign positive weight to image keys and use their values even when its own projection matrices are separate. With all scalar query–key scores zero, the permitted attention weights are uniform. In the fixture $[T,I,I,T]$, the last text position assigns half its attention mass to the image positions. Changing the image branch’s values changes that text output. Separate matrices have not isolated the modalities.

Deterministic branch selection is distinct from a learned mixture-of-experts router that chooses experts based on token content. One can combine both: choose an output/task branch and use sparse experts inside it. The label supplied to the route is part of the architecture’s contract. Confusing it with a vocabulary index, input file type, or learned semantic cluster produces misleading diagrams and parameter counts.

9. Read efficiency claims with their actual denominators 24:25

MoT can have more stored parameters than a dense model while applying only one branch to each position. Under an original simplified contract with square $d\times d$ Q/K/V/O matrices and a nongated FFN $d\to h\to d$, one branch stores $P_b=4d^2+2dh$ parameters, ignoring biases and normalization. $M$ branches store $MP_b$, while projection/FFN multiplication uses $nP_b$ multiply-accumulate terms for $n$ positions if every position uses exactly one branch of the same width. The full global attention adds approximately $2n^2d$ such terms for square head totals. A multiply-accumulate count is not a FLOP count or measured time.

At $d=8,h=16,M=2,n=20$, stored parameters are $1024$, active projection/FFN work is $10{,}240$ MACs, and the two attention matrix products cost $6400$ MACs. Doubling stored branch capacity has not doubled this arithmetic. It can still increase optimizer state, parameter transfer, memory fragmentation, and branch-dispatch overhead. Imbalanced modality counts can leave small matrix multiplications underutilized. Reuse Chapter 4’s distinction between mathematical work, communication, and exposed wall-clock cost.

In the paper’s controlled Chameleon 7B setting, MoT matches dense validation losses at a $55.8\%$ training checkpoint. In the Transfusion setting, its $760$M active model gives better reported image metrics than the $1.4$B dense baseline with about half the FLOPs: Figure 11 records FID $21.145$ versus $24.688$ and caption CIDEr $0.320$ versus $0.286$. These are study-specific comparisons, not a guarantee for the maintenance workload. PDF pp. 11–13 and 20–21 give the setups; its expert-choice comparator has recorded evaluation-causality confounds.

Speech experiments extend the architecture, using a particular semantic tokenizer and training mixture. Lower speech-token loss is evidence for that distribution, not a measurement of acoustic fidelity or a warning-sound detector. Hardware matters too: the paper’s wall-clock study uses A100 instances and a specific training setup. The chapter does not convert its FLOP ratios into universal latency savings. Compare complete systems using the assistant’s actual resolution, input mixture, accuracy targets, and latency distribution.

10. Freeze a text pathway with a carefully stated preservation claim 27:27

Adding a modality need not require discarding a pretrained language model. LMFusion introduces parallel image-specific transformer modules and trains those while freezing the existing text-specific components. The shared attention operation still connects text and images. LMFusion v4, PDF pp. 4–7, lists the frozen text embedding, Q/K/V/O, normalization/FFN path, and language head. The paper’s first version used the name LlamaFusion; the pinned later version matches the lecture’s LMFusion name.

There is a precise preservation statement. For a pure-text input, suppose its tokenizer, embeddings, positional scheme, masks, every traversed layer, output head, and evaluation settings are identical before and after adding the image branch. Suppose the branch is not traversed for that input. Then the pure-text function is unchanged. The proof is induction on layers: the input states are equal; applying the same deterministic layer to equal states yields equal next states; the same output head yields equal logits. Dropout, different numerical kernels, or modified shared components can weaken bitwise equality, so specify evaluation and numerical tolerance.

That theorem says nothing about mixed-context outputs. A text query can read image values from the newly trained branch. In the scalar fixture, frozen text values are $1$ and $3$. Pure-text causal attention gives the final value $2$ both before and after an image-branch change. In $[T,I,I,T]$, image values $2,2$ give final mean $2$; changing them to $10,10$ gives $6$. The text parameters stayed fixed while its mixed-context output changed. This is desirable cross-modal adaptation, not a violation of the pure-text statement.

For the assistant, test legacy manual-only behavior separately from photo-conditioned behavior. A general benchmark’s unchanged text score does not establish equality for every deployment prompt, and improved caption CIDEr does not establish correct revision handling. The LMFusion comparison also reuses substantial prior text pretraining: its adaptation FLOPs exclude that earlier investment. “Half the FLOPs” needs that lifecycle boundary, the image-data comparison, and the exact reported metric.

11. A visual input can belong to an understanding branch 30:13

The question period clarifies an ambiguity in the preceding story: the useful specialization is often by output task, not simply the type of the input file. An image supplied for captioning and an image being generated are both visual data, yet their representations and objectives need different capabilities. The former should preserve semantic evidence for answering; the latter must support producing pixels, including texture and geometry.

Therefore do not infer that all image positions must route to the “image generation” parameters. The lecture’s discussion of separating branches principally emphasizes generation efficiency. It does not establish that this alone improves every visual-understanding task. A task may use semantic visual features in an understanding branch and continuous noised latents in a generation branch. The next architecture makes that distinction explicit.

For the maintenance assistant, the incoming component photograph belongs to the evidence pathway. A generated annotated diagram belongs to a prediction pathway. Even when both eventually enter the same attention space, their provenance should remain distinct. If the model later treats its own diagram as evidence that the original component has a particular clip, it can create a self-confirming error. Keep the observed image, the generated illustration, and the cited manual separately identifiable.

One practical experiment is to hold the language backbone and data fixed while comparing visual features specialized for semantics, reconstruction, or both. Measure exact part recognition and exact text reading independently of generated-image appearance. That design is an original proposed test, not an experiment reported by Lin. Its purpose is to turn a naming ambiguity into falsifiable architectural alternatives.

12. BAGEL uses two visual representations and two task branches 31:38

BAGEL’s MoT design routes text and semantic ViT image tokens through an understanding expert, and VAE tokens through a generation expert. Both use shared multimodal self-attention. Its semantic encoder is initialized from SigLIP2; its generation representation uses a frozen VAE. Its visual generation objective uses rectified flow, so it should not be described as implementing exactly the Transfusion DDPM objective just because both operate on continuous latents. BAGEL v1, PDF pp. 3–6, identifies these branches, encoders, and objectives.

This distinction also changes masking and context management. BAGEL can represent an image using clean VAE tokens, noisy VAE tokens for the generation loss, and ViT tokens for understanding. Its generalized causal policy governs which version subsequent outputs may see. The simple one-block fixture earlier explains Transfusion-style image-local attention; it is not a complete implementation of BAGEL’s clean/noisy copies, image grouping, or diffusion-forcing strategy. Reusing the toy mask without those labels would lose part of the real contract.

Intermediate text can help generation. BAGEL reports IntelligentBench scores $44.9$ without its self-generated CoT and $55.3$ with it. The benchmark has $350$ image-editing examples and uses an automated model judge; the paper’s other systems sometimes answer only subsets. This supports a bounded result about those evaluated outputs and grading rules. It does not establish that every textual reasoning trace is correct, that improved editing is the same as physical reasoning, or that the effect isolates one mechanism. PDF pp. 12 and 19 provide the metric and comparison.

In the assistant, use a generated plan to specify the desired labels and edits, then check the resulting diagram against the observed photograph and manual. Preserve exact revision numbers in a trusted overlay when required rather than assume a generative image renderer is an exact typesetting engine. That is an engineering choice informed by the task’s error costs. A single model interface can unite understanding and generation without removing the need to evaluate them separately.

13. An action modality introduces dynamics and execution semantics 34:05

Lin’s robotics example is $\pi_{0.7}$. The original paper combines a vision-language backbone, observation history, an action expert, richer textual context, episode metadata, and subgoal images. An action chunk is a short sequence of future control commands; observing the environment and executing a shorter portion of that chunk creates a feedback loop. A token that denotes a desired motor command is not the same thing as an actuator executing it successfully.

The original $\pi_{0.7}$ v1 PDF pp. 3–4 also records a training detail that prevents a loose analogy: the action expert uses a continuous flow-based objective while the vision-language backbone receives a discrete-token training signal, and action-expert gradients are blocked from flowing into the backbone under its knowledge-insulation recipe. Attending to backbone states does not imply that every loss updates every component. Its generated subgoal images come from an associated image model, rather than proving that the action expert itself is an all-purpose image generator.

For an illustrative policy, write $a_{t:t+H}\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid o_{t-T:t},C_t)$, where $o$ is observation history, $C_t$ is task/context information, and $H$ is the action horizon. This is a conditional behavior description. The physical outcome also depends on state transitions, sensing error, delay, contact dynamics, and the executed horizon. A high-quality imagined subgoal can be unreachable or unsafe under the current state.

The assistant in this chapter supplies information and diagrams. Extending it to control would require explicit action authority, limits, fresh observations, and outcome checks. The research example establishes a concrete architecture and selected empirical tasks, not a universal reliability or safety certificate. Lin’s limited robotics discussion should not be expanded into detailed performance numbers that the lecture does not examine.

14. Ask which direction of transfer an experiment tests 34:58

Training a model to generate images and training it to interpret images are related interventions, but transfer need not be symmetric. Lin describes stronger evidence for understanding features helping generation than for an automatic understanding benefit from generation training. Preserve this as a qualified assessment of current observations. It is not a theorem that image generation can never improve perception, nor a promise that any understanding loss improves generated images.

Let $U(\theta)$ denote a selected understanding metric and $G(\theta)$ a selected generation metric. Comparing a jointly trained model $\theta_{UG}$ with an understanding-only model $\theta_U$ tests a generation-to-understanding intervention only if the backbone, understanding-data exposure, compute accounting, and evaluation are controlled sufficiently to interpret $U(\theta_{UG})-U(\theta_U)$. Comparing it with a generation-only model instead tests a different direction. A positive joint-model score on both benchmarks is not by itself evidence that the tasks improved each other.

Loss gradients make the issue concrete. For a shared parameter vector, $g_U=\nabla L_U$ and $g_G=\nabla L_G$ can align or oppose. A small gradient step $\Delta\theta=-\eta(g_U+\lambda g_G)$ changes the understanding loss to first order by $-\eta(\|g_U\|^2+\lambda g_U^{\mathsf T}g_G)$. If the inner product is sufficiently negative, the generation gradient can raise the understanding loss locally. This is a differentiable local approximation under small $\eta$, not a prediction of long training dynamics. Separate parameter branches change where such interference occurs; shared attention and embeddings can still couple the tasks.

For the assistant, an ablation needs difficult part-identification examples, not just captions that can be guessed from context. Test manual-only, photo-only, and combined inputs; hold out exact component instances; distinguish OCR correctness from semantic part recognition. Lin’s explanations involving abstraction, redundant sensory input, and loss differences are hypotheses to investigate. A training curve cannot identify which of them caused a directional transfer pattern.

15. Match unified and specialized systems to the application 39:16

A unified model makes shared context and interleaved generation convenient. A specialized perception module, document parser, or exact renderer can offer useful guarantees and cost advantages for a constrained workflow. The choice depends on the workload’s information and error requirements. “Native” is not a sufficient answer to whether a particular implementation is suitable for a maintenance tool.

Consider a revision table. If the application needs exact values, a parser can retain a table structure and source identifiers while a multimodal model helps interpret the accompanying diagram. If the application needs a generated illustration, a renderer can preserve checked labels from that table. Joint attention may improve the model’s handling of the multimodal request, but the application still needs to distinguish extracted facts from proposed visual content.

Lin’s conclusion connects broad digital applications with the richer sensory and action demands of the physical world. General context processing supports many tasks, yet domains can have special observability, latency, and verification constraints. A model that interprets one photograph has not demonstrated persistent tracking; a model that generates a plausible future frame has not demonstrated a calibrated dynamics predictor.

Use three gates for the assistant. First, information retention: can the front end preserve serial digits, orientation, and defect cues? Second, conditional behavior: can the model use the correct manual and observation without future-context leakage? Third, application outcome: are the answers and diagrams accurate enough under the actual error costs? This hierarchy prevents a generation benchmark from substituting for a perception test or a deployment check.

16. Shared attention supplies a conditional interface 42:05

An audience question asks how separately parameterized pathways interact. The answer is the global attention computation, not hidden communication between independent standalone models. The branch-specific projections produce queries, keys, and values in compatible spaces; the normalized attention sum then mixes permitted values from the common sequence. The model must learn useful compatibility between these representations.

Dimension agreement is necessary and insufficient. Giving text queries and image keys the same $d_k$ makes their dot product legal. It does not ensure that the dot product measures part relevance or that the image values encode the evidence the language head needs. Initialization, training objectives, data ordering, and optimization determine the learned interface. Sharing the arithmetic creates a channel through which useful information can flow; it does not certify the content of that channel.

Global attention is also conditional. Changing a preceding photograph can change later text even if every text-specific weight is frozen. Changing a forbidden future photograph should not change the earlier prefix under the stated mask. These two facts are complementary. The first tests that the application can use visual evidence; the second tests that its training and generation use only available evidence.

21. Next-token prediction is a successful objective, not a necessity proof 55:53

The audience asks whether autoregression learns the structural relationships that multimodal reasoning needs, and whether alternatives such as discrete diffusion can be useful. 25. Executable finite checks

It checks a contiguous-block attention policy, useful cross-modal attention, text-loss gradients, quantization information loss, a frozen-text counterexample, Gaussian denoising regression, and a declared parameter/cost model. It does not train a transformer, sample a diffusion process, perform OCR, or reproduce a paper’s benchmark.

The patch count includes right/bottom padding and independent frames. The scalar attention uses $d_k=d_v=1$, so its score scaling is one. The cost model uses nongated FFNs; real SwiGLU, normalization, biases, grouped-query attention, and encoders change the count. Invalid-input rejection is part of the example’s contract. Assertions on finite results are useful diagnostics, with exact real-arithmetic proofs supplied separately above.

"""Finite multimodal architecture checks; no trained model or diffusion sampler.

Run with Python 3.10+ and the standard library. Dimensions and costs describe
the explicit toy contracts below, not any cited model's complete implementation.
"""
import math
from fractions import Fraction


def positive_integer(value, name):
    if isinstance(value, bool) or not isinstance(value, int) or value <= 0:
        raise ValueError(name + ' must be a positive integer')
    return value


def finite(value, name):
    if isinstance(value, bool) or not isinstance(value, (int, float)):
        raise ValueError(name + ' must be a finite real number')
    if not math.isfinite(value):
        raise ValueError(name + ' must be a finite real number')
    return float(value)


def patches(height, width, patch, frames=1):
    """Independent frames, spatial right/bottom padding, no special tokens."""
    height = positive_integer(height, 'height')
    width = positive_integer(width, 'width')
    patch = positive_integer(patch, 'patch')
    frames = positive_integer(frames, 'frames')
    return ((height + patch - 1) // patch) * ((width + patch - 1) // patch) * frames


def hybrid_mask(blocks):
    """None = text; each integer identifies ONE contiguous image block.

    Text attends causally. Image patches also attend within their image block.
    No packed independent examples, special tokens or duplicated clean/noisy
    copies are represented by this toy mask.
    """
    if not isinstance(blocks, (list, tuple)) or not blocks:
        raise ValueError('blocks must be a nonempty sequence')
    seen, previous = set(), None
    for block in blocks:
        if block is not None:
            if isinstance(block, bool) or not isinstance(block, int) or block < 0:
                raise ValueError('image block IDs must be nonnegative integers')
            if block != previous:
                if block in seen:
                    raise ValueError('each image block must be contiguous')
                seen.add(block)
        previous = block
    return [[j <= i or (block is not None and block == blocks[j])
             for j in range(len(blocks))] for i, block in enumerate(blocks)]


def softmax(values):
    if not isinstance(values, (list, tuple)) or not values:
        raise ValueError('softmax needs a nonempty sequence')
    values = [finite(x, 'score') for x in values]
    maximum = max(values)
    weights = [math.exp(x - maximum) for x in values]
    normalizer = math.fsum(weights)
    return [x / normalizer for x in weights]


def attend(queries, keys, values, mask):
    """One scalar head (d_k=d_v=1); arbitrary finite Q/K/V projections."""
    if not queries or not (len(queries) == len(keys) == len(values) == len(mask)):
        raise ValueError('Q/K/V/mask lengths must match and be nonzero')
    n = len(queries)
    queries = [finite(x, 'query') for x in queries]
    keys = [finite(x, 'key') for x in keys]
    values = [finite(x, 'value') for x in values]
    outputs, weights = [], []
    for i, row in enumerate(mask):
        if len(row) != n or any(type(x) is not bool for x in row) or not any(row):
            raise ValueError('mask rows need n booleans and a permitted key')
        indices = [j for j, permitted in enumerate(row) if permitted]
        local = softmax([queries[i] * keys[j] for j in indices])
        full = [0.0] * n
        for j, weight in zip(indices, local):
            full[j] = weight
        weights.append(full)
        outputs.append(math.fsum(weight * values[j] for j, weight in zip(indices, local)))
    return outputs, weights


def quantize(value, codebook):
    """Nearest scalar prototype; true ties select the lowest codebook index.

    Compare exact rational distances of the validated binary floats. Ordinary
    float subtraction can overflow or round distinct distances to a false tie.
    This finite teaching lookup prioritizes correctness over lookup throughput.
    """
    value = finite(value, 'value')
    if not isinstance(codebook, (list, tuple)) or not codebook:
        raise ValueError('codebook must be a nonempty sequence')
    codebook = [finite(x, 'prototype') for x in codebook]
    exact_value = Fraction(value)
    return min(range(len(codebook)),
               key=lambda i: abs(exact_value - Fraction(codebook[i])))


def binary_text_loss(feature, coefficient, target):
    """One TEXT target logit a*u; u may have come from an image encoder.

    Returns stable binary cross entropy and its derivative with respect to u.
    There is no image reconstruction loss here.
    """
    feature = finite(feature, 'feature')
    coefficient = finite(coefficient, 'coefficient')
    if type(target) is not int or target not in (0, 1):
        raise ValueError('target must be integer 0 or 1')
    logit = finite(feature * coefficient, 'logit')
    probability = (1 / (1 + math.exp(-logit)) if logit >= 0
                   else math.exp(logit) / (1 + math.exp(logit)))
    loss = max(logit, 0) - target * logit + math.log1p(math.exp(-abs(logit)))
    return loss, coefficient * (probability - target)


def gaussian_noise_predictor(noisy_value, alpha, clean_variance):
    """Analytic E[epsilon|x_tau] for independent zero-mean scalar Gaussians.

    epsilon has variance 1; clean x_0 has the supplied positive variance.
    x_tau = sqrt(alpha)*x_0 + sqrt(1-alpha)*epsilon.
    This is a denoising-regression oracle, NOT a reverse-process sampler.
    """
    noisy_value = finite(noisy_value, 'noisy_value')
    alpha = finite(alpha, 'alpha')
    clean_variance = finite(clean_variance, 'clean_variance')
    if not 0 < alpha < 1 or clean_variance <= 0:
        raise ValueError('need 0 < alpha < 1 and clean_variance > 0')
    variance = alpha * clean_variance + 1 - alpha
    coefficient = math.sqrt(1 - alpha) / variance
    residual_mse = alpha * clean_variance / variance
    return coefficient * noisy_value, residual_mse


def parameter_cost(width, hidden, modalities, tokens):
    """Square Q/K/V/O + NON-GATED two-matrix FFN; biases/norms omitted.

    All modalities use the same widths; exactly one branch per token.
    Counts matrix multiply-accumulate terms, not FLOPs or measured latency.
    """
    for value, name in [(width, 'width'), (hidden, 'hidden'),
                        (modalities, 'modalities'), (tokens, 'tokens')]:
        positive_integer(value, name)
    branch = 4 * width * width + 2 * width * hidden
    return branch * modalities, branch * tokens, 2 * tokens * tokens * width


def invalid_contracts():
    tests = [lambda: patches(0, 10, 2), lambda: patches(True, 10, 2),
             lambda: patches(10, 10, 2.5), lambda: patches(10, 10, 2, -1),
             lambda: hybrid_mask([]), lambda: hybrid_mask([0, None, 0]),

             lambda: gaussian_noise_predictor(1, 1, 1),
             lambda: gaussian_noise_predictor(1, .5, 0),
             lambda: parameter_cost(8, 16, 0, 20),

26. Exercises and complete solutions

1. Account for image and video context

An image is $513\times769$ pixels. A tokenizer pads the bottom/right to multiples of $16$, uses independent frames, and inserts two boundary positions per image. A prompt has $600$ text positions. Find the sequence length for one image and for eight frames. Compute an explicitly materialized one-head score matrix in BF16. Then explain what this calculation does and does not say about an efficient attention implementation.

Solution

The padded grid is $\lceil513/16\rceil\times\lceil769/16\rceil=33\times49=1617$ patch positions. Including two boundaries gives $1619$ per frame. One image gives $n=600+1619=2219$. Eight independent frames give $n=600+8(1619)=13{,}552$. Squaring gives $2219^2=4{,}923{,}961$ and $13{,}552^2=183{,}656{,}704$. At two bytes per element, the matrices occupy $9{,}847{,}922$ bytes ($9.39171$ MiB) and $367{,}313{,}408$ bytes ($350.29736$ MiB).

The ratio is approximately $37.30$, not eight, because the complete sequence length is squared and includes the fixed text prefix. This is per head and per example, with no batch or layer multiplier. It assumes dense explicit storage and ignores gradients, Q/K/V, feed-forward states, and temporary buffers. An efficient attention kernel can avoid storing this matrix while still processing permitted pairs. Temporal compression or fewer visual positions changes both the sequence count and arithmetic. Boundary positions were stated explicitly here; the lab’s simpler count omits them.

2. Prove a representation can preserve semantics and lose exactness

Four observations occur uniformly: $(C,R)\in\{0,1\}^2$, where $C$ is component type and $R$ is a revision bit. An encoder retains only $Z=C$. Determine the minimum error for predicting $C$, for predicting $R$, and for reconstructing the exact pair. Give the cross-entropy lower bound for the lost revision bit and explain the maintenance implication.

Solution

Predicting $C$ from $Z$ has zero error because the representation equals the target. Conditional on either value of $Z$, the bit $R$ is uniform. Thus its minimum classification error is $1/2$. Any deterministic reconstruction picks one revision for each component type and succeeds on two of the four pairs, so its exact-pair error is also $1/2$. Randomization cannot improve either result because the conditional probabilities remain $1/2$.

For a model assigning revision probability $q$ to $R=1$, expected conditional negative log likelihood is $-\tfrac12\log q-\tfrac12\log(1-q)$. Differentiation gives $-1/(2q)+1/[2(1-q)]$, which vanishes at $q=1/2$; the second derivative is positive. The minimum is $\log2$ nats. Assigning zero probability to either observed bit gives infinite loss. The encoder is sufficient for component type and insufficient for exact revision. Excellent component recognition cannot justify a revision-specific answer. Preserve that bit in another channel or obtain a new observation; do not infer that more downstream reasoning can recover it from $Z$ alone.

3. Repair an attention mask and its leakage test

For $[T,I_0,I_0,T,I_1,I_1,T]$, a programmer allows every image query to read every image key, regardless of block. Identify a forbidden edge. With scalar zero Q/K scores and values $[1,2,4,8,16,32,64]$, compute the correct output at query $1$. Show how the faulty edge policy can make an earlier output depend on a future image. Explain why a test that only changes future values with zero image value projection could miss the bug.

Solution

Query $1$ belongs to image $I_0$. It may read indices $0,1,2$, including the later patch $2$ in its own block, but cannot read $4$ or $5$ in $I_1$. The correct uniform-attention output is $(1+2+4)/3=7/3$. The faulty policy lets it read $0,1,2,4,5$, giving $(1+2+4+16+32)/5=11$.

If future image values become $160,320$, the correct output remains $7/3$, while the faulty output becomes $(1+2+4+160+320)/5=97.4$. This is a direct future-image dependency. A useful test needs nonzero distinguishable values on the forbidden positions. If the image value projection happens to output zero for both versions, the leaked attention may have no effect in that fixture. Therefore inspect the permitted-edge set and test both forbidden changes and permitted communication. In multiple layers, a future-block edge can contaminate states that later appear causally legitimate; the block-order argument, not a single fortunate output, supplies the general boundary.

4. Derive a weighted objective and its uncertainty boundary

A batch has $100$ text targets with summed cross-entropy $80$ nats and $20$ images, each with $16$ latent coordinates, whose total noise-prediction squared error is $160$. Define text mean per target and image mean per coordinate. With $\lambda=2$, compute the combined loss. Then compute it if image loss is instead a mean of per-image summed errors. Finally derive the optimal noise predictor and residual MSE for independent $x_0\sim\mathcal N(0,4)$, $\epsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,1)$, $x_\tau=\sqrt{1/4}x_0+\sqrt{3/4}\epsilon$.

Solution

The text mean is $80/100=0.8$. The image coordinate mean is $160/(20\times16)=0.5$. Therefore $L=0.8+2(0.5)=1.8$. With per-image summed errors, $L_I=160/20=8$ and $L=0.8+2(8)=16.8$. Neither is intrinsically the correct reduction. The coefficient must be understood with its declared reduction; the second image gradient contribution is sixteen times the first at the same $\lambda$. This illustrates why copying a coefficient from another implementation can change learning substantially.

Here $\operatorname{Var}(x_\tau)=(1/4)4+3/4=7/4$ and $\operatorname{Cov}(\epsilon,x_\tau)=\sqrt3/2$. The Gaussian conditional mean is $\mathbb E[\epsilon\mid x_\tau]=(2\sqrt3/7)x_\tau$. Its conditional variance is $1-(3/4)/(7/4)=4/7$. By the squared-error decomposition, that conditional mean is optimal and $4/7$ is the irreducible MSE. It is larger than the lab’s $1/4$ because the clean prior variance here is four rather than one. This analytic predictor is not a sampler and does not claim a trained model attains the bound.

5. Count active work and delimit frozen behavior

A toy layer has $d=32$, nongated FFN width $h=64$, three modality branches, and $n=100$ positions. Ignore biases and norms. Count stored Q/K/V/O+FFN parameters, active projection/FFN MACs, and dense-attention MACs. State sufficient conditions for pure-text function preservation after training only a new branch. Construct a mixed-context counterexample with frozen text values $1,3$ and two image values changed from $2,2$ to $10,10$.

Solution

Each branch has $4(32)^2+2(32)(64)=8192$ parameters. Three branches store $24{,}576$. Exactly one branch of the same width per position gives $100(8192)=819{,}200$ projection/FFN MACs. Global QK and AV products give $2(100)^2(32)=640{,}000$ MACs. Stored parameters triple relative to one branch while these active arithmetic counts remain the same. There is no inference that memory, communication, routing overhead, or wall-clock time remains unchanged.

Pure-text preservation follows when the tokenizer, position scheme, masks, input embeddings, all traversed layers, normalization, head, and deterministic evaluation settings remain fixed, and the new branch is not traversed. Layerwise induction gives equal states and logits. If a shared normalization or head is updated, those sufficient conditions fail. For the mixed sequence $[T,I,I,T]$ with zero scalar scores and all previous positions allowed, the final text output changes from $(1+2+2+3)/4=2$ to $(1+10+10+3)/4=6$. The text parameters are unchanged, but conditioning values are different. This is why a frozen-text claim must specify pure-text inputs.

6. Design a transfer experiment and a timing gate

You want to know whether diagram-generation training improves the assistant’s revision recognition. Specify a controlled ablation, the primary metric, and a failure that would make a positive score uninterpretable. The interactive system receives one chunk every $0.2$ seconds and needs $0.24$ seconds of serial service per chunk. Find the deterministic backlog after $50$ chunks. Explain why a $200$ ms model unit and a faster mean service time are insufficient to certify the application.

Solution

Start from the same pretrained checkpoint and compare revision-recognition training alone with the same training plus a declared generation objective. Match understanding-data exposure, component split, and hyperparameter selection; record the added compute and representation changes. One option holds total compute fixed and reports the resulting exposure difference explicitly; a second holds understanding exposure fixed and reports extra generation cost. Neither comparison can silently claim both are fixed when they are not.

Use exact revision accuracy on held-out component instances as the primary metric, with OCR/part-identification submetrics and uncertainty or abstention rates. Evaluate manual-only, photo-only, and combined inputs. Prevent train/test duplicate images, revision metadata leakage, and captions that directly reveal the answer. A positive score from extra understanding examples, a changed tokenizer, or contaminated test images cannot identify generation-to-understanding transfer. Generated-image quality should be reported separately.

Starting from zero, the specified backlog recursion adds $0.24-0.20=0.04$ seconds per chunk, so after $50$ chunks it is $2$ seconds. This excludes initial chunk collection and other delays. A $200$ ms unit does not bound end-to-end latency; network delay, scheduling, service variance, background completion, and output buffering matter. Even with mean service below $0.2$, bursts or heavy tails can violate response deadlines. Test tail latency and stale-result rejection using manual revision/observation identifiers. A timely response about the wrong revision fails the application despite satisfying a throughput target.
