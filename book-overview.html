Learning route and prerequisites

Transformers United V6 brings together nine talks about model architecture, learning, generalization and deployment. This independent reading edition follows their Spring 2026 order. Each chapter will distinguish the recording's claims from primary research and original teaching examples.

The intended reader knows linear algebra, differentiation and elementary probability. Familiarity with Python helps with the numerical examples. The opening chapter develops the vocabulary needed to distinguish a model's architecture, training objective, context and generated output.

  1. Overview of transformers. Establish the attention mechanism and examine how data, post-training and inference change the behavior of an assistant.
  2. Representation learning and world modeling. Examine what a learned representation preserves and what a model must predict.
  3. State space models and transformers. Compare the architectural tradeoffs rather than treating sequence models as interchangeable.
  4. Training at GPU scale. Connect computation, memory and communication when distributing training.
  5. Beyond next-token prediction. Examine the relationship between a training objective and the capabilities attributed to a model.
  6. Generalization from parameters and context. Separate what training stores from what an inference-time prompt supplies.
  7. Collaborative scientific and medical agents. Examine delegation, evidence and validation in specialized workflows.
  8. Native multimodal intelligence. Consider how a model represents and relates different input modalities.
  9. Serving transformers. Connect model behavior with the resource and latency constraints of deployment.
